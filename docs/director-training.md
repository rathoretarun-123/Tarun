# Pixar-style directing guide — how the director applies it

`ShotPlanner` turns the guide into decisions for every line; `Director` stages them; `qualityCheck()` checks the
result and writes the shot list (in the guide's §47 format) that comes with every film.

- **§1, §49 Character first:** shot choice starts from the line's emotion and intensity (feeling, manner, shouts, realisations).
- **§2, §16, §44 Establish and the arc:** each part opens on a wide, readable establishing shot (extreme wide for grand places); lines are placed on an arc: introduce, develop, escalate, peak, release.
- **§3, §4, §41, §42 Shot size follows emotion:** wider when calm, closer as feelings grow, an extreme close-up only at a strong peak, back out on release; sizes are measured from each character's height.
- **§5 Camera height:** eye level normally, high angle for fear and sadness, low angle for menace and heroic peaks; the background shifts with the angle.
- **§6, §17, §18 Screen position:** look room, thirds, a character alone with a heavy feeling small at the edge of an empty frame.
- **§7, §8 Two-shots and over-the-shoulder:** relationships in two-shots, conversations over the shoulder.
- **§9, §10, §11, §28, §31 Reactions:** after strong lines the listener's face is shown and the silence is held; listeners react a beat late, nod, look away and back.
- **§13, §14, §43 Camera restraint:** strong performances get a still camera (with the Technical Director protocol every shot is static).
- **§15 Reveals:** a giant or monster is revealed feet first, then the whole figure.
- **§19 Light:** soft warm light for warmth, harder directional light and deeper shadows for conflict and fear.
- **§23, §24 Anticipation and follow-through:** reactions after a pause, crouch before jumps, hair and hems settle after moves.
- **§26 Body language:** each character's energy and poise come from the description (lively, calm, proud, shy).
- **§32 Editing rhythm:** calm conversations hold a framing; action scenes cut more.
- **§34 Transitions:** dissolves between scenes, a dip to black when time passes, a dip to white for dreams, memories and magic.
- **§48 Quality check:** readable openings, no jump cuts, close-up count, static shots, reactions, two-shots, pacing.
