# Writing a script the app reads right

The way you asked scripts to be written: **every character, place and thing listed at the start, and every line of
dialogue in double quotes.** The reader takes many other layouts too (below), but this one is read the most surely.

## The layout

```
शीर्षक: बारिश वाला रविवार

पात्र परिचय
1. तरुण - 42 साल, पिता
रूप: हल्की दाढ़ी, नीली टी-शर्ट
2. प्रिया - 40 साल, माँ
रूप: सूती कुर्ता, बाल जुड़े में

जगह का परिचय
1. घर का लिविंग रूम - सोफा, TV, खिड़की
2. सोसाइटी का पार्क - हरी घास, झूला, स्लाइड

वस्तुएँ
1. छाता - लाल रंग का बड़ा छाता
2. गेंद - लाल रबर की गेंद

---

दृश्य 1: घर का लिविंग रूम - सुबह

[प्रिया रसोई में चाय बनाती है। तरुण सोफे पर बैठकर अखबार पढ़ता है।]

प्रिया (मुस्कुराते हुए): "चाय तैयार है!"

तरुण: "आ रहा हूँ।"
```

* **The title** — `शीर्षक:`, `फिल्म का नाम:`, `कहानी:`, `Title:`.
* **The characters** — under `पात्र परिचय`, `पात्र`, `कलाकार`, `चरित्र परिचय`, `भूमिका`, `Characters`, `Cast`,
  `Starring`, `Who's who` … one per line, numbered or with bullets: name, age, relation; then `रूप:` / `Look:` and
  `स्वभाव:` / `Nature:` lines. Only the characters listed here are cast (a name met later is not a new character).
* **The places** — under `जगह का परिचय`, `स्थान`, `स्थल`, `जगहें`, `पृष्ठभूमि`, `सेट्स`, `Places`, `Locations`,
  `Sets`, `Backgrounds` …
* **The things** — under `वस्तुएँ`, `वस्तु`, `चीज़ें`, `सामग्री`, `उपकरण`, `Props`, `Objects`, `Things`, `Equipment` …
* **Where the script begins** — a line `---`, `स्क्रिप्ट शुरू`, `कहानी शुरू`, `आरंभ`, `FADE IN`, `Script begins` (or just
  the first scene heading).
* **Scenes** — `दृश्य 1: …`, `सीन 2 - …`, `दृश्य एक`, `पहला दृश्य`, `Scene 3`, `Scene three`, `Act I`, `भाग 2`,
  `अध्याय 3`, `Episode 1`, or a screenplay slugline (`INT. LIVING ROOM - DAY`) when there are no numbered scenes. After
  the colon or dash: the place, then the time of day.
* **What happens** — in square brackets or round brackets, or a plain line: `[वनुशा पानी के गड्ढे में कूदती है।]`.
* **Dialogue** — the name, an optional manner in brackets, a colon or dash, and the words **in double quotes**. Every
  double quote is read: `"…"`, `“…”`, `„…“`, `«…»`, `''…''`, `〝…〞`, `＂…＂`. A line too long for one line may go on
  to the next line until the quote closes. Screenplay form works too: the name alone on a line, an optional
  `(manner)` line, then the words. `(V.O.)` or `(O.S.)` after a name is a voice heard but not seen.

## Everyday words

The director stages what the direction says: chores (tea, sweeping, mopping, washing, cooking, reading, writing,
the phone, combing, watering plants), eating and drinking, sitting, lying, sleeping and waking, walking in, out,
across, slowly, on tiptoe, running after someone, the stairs; cycles, bikes, scooters, autos, the car and the bus;
the swing, the slide, the see-saw; exercise; hugs, held hands, touching the feet, folding hands, a push, a gift;
feelings — a smile, a laugh, tears, anger, fear, sadness, shyness, surprise, thought, pride, relief, pain, tiredness;
and many small things — a head scratch, a nod, a sneeze, a yawn, a wink, a whistle, a song, looking up at the sky,
searching, hiding, kneeling, a fall and getting up, a kite, feeding the birds, patting a dog, a door, a light, a lock,
a photo, a cake, an umbrella. `tools/testdata/v41vocab` holds 189 such sentences; each is checked to stage something.

## What it cannot do

It reads the words of a script; it does not understand a story the way a person does. A direction it has no words for
gets at least the feeling it names, or nothing. The characters are your pictures on a fine mesh (flat pictures, not 3D
models): from the front picture they turn to about three-quarter; a full profile (walking across) and the back (turned
away) show only when you give a standing side picture and a back picture of the same character.
